02 · Data sources

Five sources, feeding one hub

Four sources update once a day; one (RB2B) updates the moment something happens. The dashboard reads from one central place that all five feed into, so it stays fast no matter how much data is behind it.

GoHighLevel — Live

Contacts, opportunities, and form submissions, updated daily. Powers most of the dashboard.

Google Analytics 4 — Live

Website visits and traffic sources, updated daily. Powers the Funnel and Engagement panels.

Google Ads — Live

Spend and performance, updated daily. Powers Paid Traffic and Ad Spend·ROAS.

LinkedIn Ads — Live

Spend and performance. LinkedIn doesn't offer a live connection yet, so this comes from a report we re-upload as fresh ones come in.

RB2B — Live, real time

Identifies people and companies visiting your website, even when they don't fill out a form — updated the instant it happens, not on a daily schedule like the rest. A one-time import also brought in RB2B's past activity, so the history goes back further than just launch day.

A note on "Marketing-Attributed"

Some panels let you toggle between all opportunities and just the marketing-driven ones. That toggle counts a lead as "marketing" if they came from a form or an email sequence — not from an ad click or a general website visit, since those turned out to be too broad to count as real marketing credit.

04 · Known limitations

What to keep in mind when reading these numbers

None of these are bugs — just things worth knowing so a number is never misread.

Ad Spend · ROAS can look off over short time windows

It compares spend and revenue within the same date range — but a deal that closes this week might've started weeks or months ago. Over a week this can look extreme; over 90 days or a year, it evens out and means more.

Some panels show "right now," not history

Opportunity Velocity, Pipeline Stage Breakdown, and Lead Source Breakdown show where things stand today — not everyone who ever passed through a stage. GoHighLevel doesn't keep that fuller history.

Lead Source Breakdown is still being checked

It's new, and early numbers show more "Other" than expected. We're confirming it against real records before treating it as final.

RB2B doesn't show ad campaigns yet

RB2B's own dashboard can show which ad campaign brought a visitor in — this integration can't yet, since that detail isn't in the data it sends us.

05 · Access & privacy

How the dashboard is protected, in plain terms

Charts show totals only

Every chart and number on the dashboard shows totals and trends — no individual names or contact details.

Clicking in shows real names

Clicking into a chart to see the list behind it does show real names, emails, and phone numbers — to anyone signed in with the dashboard's shared password. We made this trade-off on purpose: full detail was worth more than extra masking, since the dashboard is only reachable by team members who already have that password.

Look, don't touch

Nothing on the dashboard can edit a campaign, change a budget, or touch a record in GHL, Google, or LinkedIn. The one exception is RB2B, which needs to send us new visitor data as it happens — it still can't change or delete anything.

Two separate places for data

The numbers on the charts live in one place, shared by link. Individual contact and opportunity details live somewhere else entirely — only reachable through the dashboard's own login, never shared publicly.
